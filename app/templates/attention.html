02How it works
  • Quick version: ten trials. Each one waits a random 1.5 to 4 seconds, so the rhythm cannot be anticipated, then lights up.
  • 3-minute version: it follows the design of NASA's Reaction Self Test, a 3-minute psychomotor vigilance test flown on the International Space Station. Random waits of 1 to 4 seconds for three minutes; a response over 355 ms, or none, is a lapse (the convention for the 3-minute test, Basner and Dinges 2011). We do not claim equivalence with NASA's software, only the same design.
  • Responding before the light, or less than 100 ms after it (too fast to be a reaction to it), counts as too early and restarts that trial's wait.
  • No response within 1.5 seconds of the light counts as a miss.
  • Only five numbers are stored: the median reaction time of valid trials, the trial count, the too-early and missed counts, and the lapse count. Escape cancels a run without sending anything.
  • Illustrative reaction-time check. Not a medical test. It is context for a human reviewer and has no threshold or score.
